Buyer intent map

Trade buyers usually arrive with a specific job, not a browsing mindset.

The page has to prove trust quickly, split services clearly and move the visitor towards a quote before they compare three more trades.

Emergency or repair

A fast yes/no on whether you handle the job.

Put phone, enquiry and core repair services in the first screen.

Planned quote

Proof that the business handles projects like theirs.

Show service groups, project photos, accreditations and review snippets near the quote CTA.

Price checking

A sense of affordability before they send details.

Use from-prices, estimate prompts or a clear quote process instead of hiding every cost.

Trust checking

Confidence that the team is legitimate and easy to contact.

Add insurance, guarantees, trade bodies, Google reviews and recent work examples.

Buyer questions

Questions visitors need answered before they enquire.

Should a trade website list prices?

Yes, at least as guide prices, from-prices or quote bands. It reduces low-quality enquiries and gives serious buyers enough confidence to contact you.

Do trades need lots of pages?

Usually no at launch. A strong homepage, core services, project proof and a frictionless quote form beat a large thin website.

What proof matters most?

Recent job photos, reviews, qualifications, insurance, guarantees and a clear process for quotes.
